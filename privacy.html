Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 November 2026 (v6)

This policy explains what personal data Vizzie Trading Pty Ltd (ACN 701 787 902), trading as Vizzie ("we", "us"), collects when you use the Vizzie application and website, how we use it, and the choices you have. Vizzie is a tool for turning open data into maps, charts, and tables.

Data we collect

  • Account details — your email address and display name, provided when you sign up or sign in (including via Google or Microsoft).
  • Your content — the projects, maps, charts, files you upload, and the data sources you follow (open-data portals, publishers, datasets).
  • Usage & security events — sign-in events (time and browser type), the storage your downloaded data uses, and a record of exports you create.
  • Product analytics & session recordings — free plan only — while Vizzie is in beta we use PostHog to record how the app is used, including session replays, so we can see where people get stuck. This runs on the free plan only, and stops as soon as an account moves to a paid plan. The free plan is for working with public open data; paid plans are where you bring your own data, and we would rather hold no recording of that at all. Anything you type is masked — form fields, search boxes and passwords are blocked out before the recording leaves your browser. Other text on the page is recorded, so we can see which dataset you opened or what an error said. This is part of why recording is limited to the free plan: there, what is on screen is public open data. You can turn recording off at any time under your profile, and we honour your browser’s “Do Not Track” setting.
  • Website analytics and campaign attribution — on www.vizzie.org and in the app we use the same tool (PostHog) to count page visits and see which pages people read, and we record the campaign, search or link that brought you here (for example the utm_source tag on an advertisement). This is kept in a cookie on the vizzie.org domain, written by whichever of our sites you land on first, so that if you later create an account we can tell which campaign it came from; it is then stored against your account for that purpose. Session recording is not used on the marketing website — only in the app, as described above. We honour your browser’s “Do Not Track” setting here too.
  • Feedback you send us — if you use the feedback button, we keep your message and the page you were on. A screenshot you choose to attach is emailed to us and not stored.
  • Published-map visits — when someone opens a map you publish, we record the visit for your own analytics. We store a hashed (irreversible) form of the visitor's IP address, an approximate country, and the address of the page that referred them — never the raw IP.
  • The Vizzie API and AI assistants — when an AI assistant or a program uses the Vizzie API (api.vizzie.org), including through our MCP server, we receive what it sends: search words, place names and addresses, area codes, points or shapes, and, when it makes or edits a map, the map's titles and notes, and the codes and values, or the census topic and place, in it. We don't receive the rest of your conversation or your files. What the assistant's provider does with your conversation, and with the answers we return, is covered by its own privacy policy. We use the IP address a request comes from to apply limits and prevent abuse. Often this is the assistant provider's address, not yours. Our hosting provider's request logs record it. Our usage counts hold a one-way keyed hash of it or, if you are signed in, your account id.
  • What assistants look for — so that we can add and improve the data people need, we keep a record of what assistants and programs ask the API for: the words searched for, with the country, city or suburb, and state; which datasets were described or mapped; and, when a map names the dataset it was made from, the dataset's columns, boundaries and colours that were chosen. This record doesn't include your account, your IP address or the map's title and values. We leave out search words that look like an address, an email, a phone number or a link, but we can't recognise every personal detail, so please don't put any in a search.
  • Maps made through the API — a map made from a table, a census topic and place, or a catchment is stored so that its link works. Anyone who has the link can open the map and see its data, so don't send personal or sensitive data. With each map we keep a one-way keyed hash of the IP address that made it, and how often and when it was last opened. If the assistant says which dataset the map was made from, we keep that with the map. The link stops working after 30 days and the map is deleted soon after, unless someone saves it to a Vizzie account.
  • Connecting an AI assistant to your account — if you connect an assistant, such as Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini, you sign in to Vizzie and choose whether to allow it. If you do, the assistant's provider receives your account id, email address and name. The assistant can then save, edit and prepare to publish maps in your account through the API. It can't open your projects or uploads. We keep a record of the connection. A map it saves is stored against your account, doesn't expire, and is listed on your projects page, where you can remove it. Saving a map doesn't make it private: its link still opens it.

Publishing & sharing a map

When you publish a map while signed in, the map and the data behind it are stored with your account, and you can turn its link off. In older links, and maps published without signing in, the data is held in the link itself, so we can't turn those off. Either way, anyone who has the link can view that data — so treat published links as public, and do not publish maps built on personal or sensitive data.

How we use it

  • To provide the service — sign you in, save your projects, and show your usage and analytics.
  • To secure accounts and prevent abuse.
  • To improve Vizzie — to see where people get stuck, to decide which data to add, describe and fix first, and to test and improve the maps it suggests.
  • To send essential account emails (e.g. confirmation and password reset), and optional product updates you opt into.

Who we share it with

We do not sell your personal data. We use trusted service providers to run Vizzie, who process data on our behalf:

  • Supabase — authentication and database hosting for your account and content.
  • PostHog — product analytics and session replay while we are in beta.
  • Resend — delivering feedback you send us by email.
  • Google / Microsoft — only if you choose to sign in with them, to verify your identity.
  • Render — hosting for the app, the Vizzie API and the service that reads open data, and their database.
  • Cloudflare — map tiles, and preview cards for published maps.
  • Geoapify, and OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service — place names, addresses and points, to find them and to work out walking and driving areas.
  • Mapbox, and the free OSRM service — routes, when you use the routing tool.
  • Translation services — search words, to search portals in other languages. We use Microsoft Azure Translator and, if it isn't available, Google Translate.
  • Anthropic — the AI that suggests how a dataset opens and what its columns mean. It receives the dataset's title, column names, and a summary or sample of its values. For a file you upload it receives the title, column names and a summary, not the rows.
  • Sentry and Better Stack — technical reports and logs, which can include details of a request that failed.
  • An AI assistant you connect — as described above, and only if you allow it.
  • Open-data providers you connect to serve the public datasets you map.

Data retention

We keep your account data for as long as your account is active. Raw map-visit records are rolled up into aggregate counts after about 30 days, and old sign-in records are pruned over time. Maps made through the API are kept for 30 days or, once saved to an account, until you remove them or delete your account. After an account is deleted, its saved maps are deleted, and its id is removed from our usage counts, within about 10 days.

Search words sent through the API are kept for 90 days; after that only monthly totals by country remain. Counts of which datasets were used, and how they were mapped, don't say who used them and have no end date. Walking and driving areas we have worked out are kept without anything linking them to the person who asked. Server logs and error reports are kept for up to 30 days. The backups of the API's database go back 7 days, so a deleted record can remain in them for that long.

You can delete your account and data at any time — see Data Deletion.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export, or delete your personal data, and to withdraw consent. To exercise these, contact us at the address below or follow the deletion instructions.

You can end an assistant's connection under Connected apps in your profile. It may keep working for up to an hour. Maps it saved stay until you remove them, and removing a saved map stops its link. A map you keep or add to a project stays there until you delete it. While the assistant's map exists, that layer follows the changes your assistant makes, and you can undo them. The record of what assistants look for doesn't say who asked, so we can't find or remove one person's entries in it.

Security

Access to your data is restricted so that you can only reach your own records; passwords are handled by our authentication provider and are never stored by us in plain text.

Children

Vizzie is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their data.
